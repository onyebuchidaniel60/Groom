# Groom — Product Research and Evidence Ledger

**Prepared:** 1 November 2026  
**Status:** Concept selected by operator; novelty gate NOT READY. This is not a claim of market demand or global originality.

## 1. Concept fingerprint

- **User/context:** Men's grooming brands, barber chains and independent barbers deciding which proposed haircut or beard-style collection to launch or promote.
- **Job:** Compare several candidate styles with adult target consumers before committing to a collection.
- **Mechanism:** Same participant photo, same fixed collection, YouCam renders each option, randomised display order, structured rating for each and one first-choice vote, aggregate-only reporting.
- **Data/capability:** One participant selfie, preset hairstyle or beard templates, consent, ratings and self-reported hair texture (optional only for hairstyle study).
- **Interaction:** Brand creates/publishes one study; participant completes one anonymous short session; brand receives aggregate results.
- **Outcome:** Directional evidence of style preference among respondents.

## 3. Three required novelty passes (must be completed before any public originality claim)

### Pass A — semantic/problem search
Search and document:
- `men's grooming consumer panel virtual haircut preference testing`
- `barbershop launch new haircut styles customer concept test`
- `pre launch beard style collection consumer research`
- `virtual hairstyle consumer research panel brand testing`
- `customer preference research haircuts before barbershop menu launch`
- Synonyms: hairstyle concept testing, barber style vote, virtual barber focus group, grooming collection validation, hair-style market research.

### Pass B — mechanism/workflow search
Search and compare:
- Same participant selfie applied to every candidate template.
- Multiple virtual hairstyle/beard alternatives within one controlled study.
- Randomised style order, rating each variant and first-choice selection.
- Grouped results by self-reported hair characteristics.
- Pre-manufacture or pre-menu selection dashboard.
- Search source families: product documentation/demos, app stores, product directories, commercial research panels, GitHub, Devpost and patents/public disclosures where useful.

### Pass C — adversarial nearest-neighbour search
Assume an equivalent already exists. Search barber chains, franchise menu testing, CPG product concept testing, virtual try-on panels, haircut simulator + research dashboard, white-label VTO user-testing software, salon merchandising analytics and agency tools. Inspect the strongest matches and identify whether a user could reproduce Groom with an existing vendor plus survey software.

For each pass, save actual query strings, date, URLs opened, inspected content, overlap, gaps and changed conclusion. Do not mark a pass complete merely because a search page was opened.

The potential differentiator—controlled multi-respondent, same-photo comparison with aggregate-only selection findings—has not yet been exhaustively searched against research-panel and VTO platforms. ## 5. Problem evidence limitations

The workflow's business problem is plausible but no customer interviews or first-party usage evidence have been collected in this project. No market size, willingness-to-pay, adoption, retention or demand metrics are claimed. Validate with a small number of grooming business interviews before post-hackathon commercialisation.

## 6. Recommended initial customer discovery questions

These are for later validation; they are not a reason to expand the MVP:
1. How do you currently decide which new haircut/beard styles to add to your menu or promote?
2. Tell me about the most recent time customer preference differed from your team's expectation.
3. What evidence do you currently collect before launching a new style?
4. Would a virtual comparison using customer photos change the decision? Why or why not?
5. What would make the output credible enough to influence a launch decision?
6. Who would own this workflow and what budget/process would it replace?

Do not treat positive hypothetical answers as demonstrated demand; seek observed behaviour, prior spend and willingness to run a pilot.
